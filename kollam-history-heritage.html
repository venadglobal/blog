The Forgotten Empire: Did You
Know the Real History of Kollam?

"THEEYIL KURUTHATHU VEYILATHU VADILLA"
(Born in fire, won't fade in sunlight)

✍️ Antony Ancil — Kollam, Kerala 📅 August 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read

🌊 The Ashtamudi Advantage

Kollam is blessed with Ashtamudi Lake — meaning "Eight Cones," or branches. This massive, deep-water estuary acts like a gigantic natural sponge for the district. When the Kallada River swells from heavy monsoon rains, Ashtamudi Lake absorbs the overflow and channels it safely into the Arabian Sea at Neendakara — a flawless natural drainage system that prevents the water from backing up into the city streets.

⛰️ The Topography

Unlike Kuttanad (Alappuzha), which sits below sea level, or Ernakulam, heavily concreted over its natural wetlands, Kollam has a naturally undulating topography. The terrain guides rainwater toward the backwaters and the sea rather than letting it pool in residential areas.

🛶 Ancient Canal Architecture

The Venad kings and later administrators built a brilliant, interconnected network of canals — including the historic TS Canal. These weren't just for trade; they were engineered to keep water flowing. While other cities filled in their canals to build shopping malls, Kollam's underlying water network still helps mitigate severe waterlogging today.

🛡️ The Ultimate Safe Haven

This geographic blessing is exactly why the fishermen of Kollam can become Kerala's first responders. Because their own homes and families in Kollam are relatively safe from floodwaters, these men have the peace of mind to take their boats, leave the district, and risk their lives to save people in Alappuzha, Pathanamthitta, and Ernakulam.

If you spend enough time on social media, you'll see a strange trend: people making memes and cracking jokes about Kollam. It's become a favorite pastime for internet trolls.

But internet trolls don't read history.

Long before the modern cities of Kerala had paved roads, Kollam — historically known as Quilon or Desinganadu — was a regional powerhouse: the commercial, cultural, and administrative epicenter of the region. There's a legendary Malayalam proverb for a reason.

"Kollam kandavanu illam venda"
(He who sees Kollam will no longer need his home)

Before anyone makes another joke about Kollam, here are the historical facts they need to know.

1️⃣ We Restarted Time: The Birth of the Malayalam Calendar

Did you know the entire state of Kerala operates on a timeline created in Kollam?

The Malayalam Calendar, known as Kollavarsham, was established in 825 AD — created right here in Kollam by the rulers of Venad to standardize trade and taxation. Kollam implemented a mathematically precise solar-lunar calendar that the entire state still uses today for festivals, agriculture, and astrology.

2️⃣ We Were Kerala's First Aviation Hub

Decades before Trivandrum or Cochin had commercial flights, Kollam had an airport.

The Quilon Aerodrome at Ashramam was among the earliest airfields in the state of Kerala, built to serve the VIPs of the Madras Presidency, the British, and the Travancore Royal Family. While the rest of the state was navigating by bullock cart and backwater boats, planes were touching down in Kollam.

A small correction for accuracy: the Quilon Aerodrome was established in the 1920s, not 1932 — 1932 is closer to when it wound down operations, roughly around the period Trivandrum's own airport came into use. Either way, the core fact stands: Kollam had a working airfield years before Kerala's other major cities did.

3️⃣ The Original "Global" Trade Center

If you want to talk about international trade, Kollam was doing it 2,000 years ago.

THE ROMANS & ARABS

Kollam was one of the most vital ports in the ancient world, actively trading spices with the Roman Empire and the Arab world.

THE CHINESE CONNECTION

The main market in Kollam is still called Chinakada — the Chinese Market. The Ming Dynasty of China traded heavily here, establishing settlements and installing the famous Chinese fishing nets (Cheena Vala) on Ashtamudi Lake.

THE WORLD'S GREATEST EXPLORERS

Legendary global travelers like Marco Polo (in 1292 AD) and Ibn Battuta visited Kollam and documented it as one of the most magnificent and wealthy port cities in the world.

4️⃣ The Cashew Capital of the World

When it comes to industrial muscle, Kollam built a major share of the modern Kerala economy. In the 20th century, Kollam transformed into the "Cashew Capital of the World." At its peak, the cashew factories of Kollam processed and exported a massive share of the global cashew supply — providing employment to hundreds of thousands of workers, bringing in foreign exchange, and helping pioneer the global food processing industry.

5️⃣ The Capital of the Venad Kingdom

Kollam was the proud capital of the Venad Kingdom — a fiercely independent and wealthy state that patronized art, literature, and diplomacy. The Venad kings were capable administrators who built infrastructure, maintained trade relationships, and kept the region wealthy and secure.

6️⃣ Kerala's Own Army: The Fishermen

When the catastrophic floods of 2018 swallowed entire districts, official rescue systems were overwhelmed fast. Before the military could fully deploy, the fishermen of Kollam were already on the move — navigating their coastal boats through submerged urban streets, rescuing an estimated 65,000 stranded citizens. The state honored them with a title earned in blood and sweat: "Kerala's Own Army."

But here's what the trolls don't know: it didn't stop in 2018. Even during recent flood distress, the fishermen of Kollam didn't wait for official orders or disaster relief funds. They've hired trucks at their own expense, loaded up their boats, and driven straight into other districts to help — risking their own lives and willingly damaging the very boats that feed their families, expecting nothing in return. When the rest of the state is drowning, Kollam consistently serves as its self-funded lifeboat.

7️⃣ The Mothers of the Cashew Industry

We often talk about Kollam being the "Cashew Capital" as a dry economic fact. The sentimental, human reality is that the modern, educated middle class of Kollam was built on the burnt, calloused fingertips of its mothers and grandmothers.

Manual cashew processing exposes hands to highly caustic cashew nutshell liquid (CNSL). For decades, lakhs of women in Kollam sat in those factories, enduring the burning pain, the harsh conditions, and the grueling hours. They didn't do it for recognition — they did it to pay school fees. The high literacy rates and successful professionals coming out of Kollam today are, in real part, the result of that immense, silent maternal sacrifice inside those factories.

8️⃣ A Sanctuary of True Brotherhood

While modern politics constantly tries to divide people along religious lines, Kollam has historically been a fortress of communal harmony. This is the city where the Sasthamcotta Sree Dharma Sastha Temple peacefully coexists with its ancient ecosystem, and where festivals of different faiths have historically supported each other. Because Kollam was built on global trade, its people learned centuries ago that collaboration, hospitality, and mutual respect are worth more than division.

The Conclusion

A city isn't defined by modern social media algorithms. It's defined by its foundational architecture and its legacy.

Kollam gave Kerala its calendar, its first airport, its first taste of globalized trade, and its strongest economic engine.

So the next time someone makes a joke about Kollam — just smile. They're standing on a timeline we created, enjoying an economy built on our trade routes.
