Autumn: The Season of Wisdom — Why Letting Go Is More Beautiful Than Holding On

A seasonal change whispers through the air. In some trees, green fades as chlorophyll breaks down, revealing other pigments — a reminder that change can reveal what was already there.

✍️ Antony Ancil — Kollam🍁 AUTUMN SERIES🔗 PHASE 03October 2026
⚠️ VGC NOTE: Phase 3 of 4. If summer was about giving, autumn asks what you need to preserve — and what you can release. Spoiler: the colors still have something to say.

In parts of North India, autumn can turn familiar trees amber, crimson, and gold. Kollam’s seasonal rhythm is different: the rains ease, the light shifts, and the landscape changes in its own way. The image of a leaf changing color is a metaphor here — a reminder that a person can reveal qualities that were present all along.

Last year. And I felt this autumn thing in my own chest. For years I was in summer — give, give, give, run the site, chase the client, collect the certificate, prove to the people who doubted me that I can write. Then one morning I woke up and I didn’t want to prove anything. I wanted to sit with a chai and look at what I already built. That is autumn. It is not tiredness. It is perspective.

1. What Actually Happens in Autumn

In trees that display autumn color, people sometimes mistake the change for sickness. In many deciduous species, however, it is part of a seasonal process. The green was not a mask; it simply dominated what the eye could see.

During the growing season, chlorophyll — the green pigment — is so dominant that it can mask other pigments. Carotenoids create yellow and orange tones and are usually present already. Anthocyanins, which can create red and purple tones, are produced in some species under particular conditions; they are not present in every leaf or every autumn.

In many deciduous trees, shorter days and changing temperatures help trigger leaf senescence. A layer of specialized cells — the abscission layer — develops near the base of the leaf stalk, eventually helping the leaf separate. As chlorophyll breaks down and is no longer replaced at the same rate, carotenoids may become more visible; some species also produce anthocyanins. The leaf is not simply painting itself gold: changing chemistry reveals colors that were hidden or newly produced.

Read that again: before a leaf falls, the tree can reclaim valuable nutrients from it. The exact process varies by species, but the principle is powerful: even during transition, something valuable can be carried forward. That is wisdom. Not “I will hold on till I die,” but “Let me offer what I can before I let go.”

2. The Human Equivalent — The Golden Years Are Not Fading Years

Many people are made to feel that after 50, they are over — that wisdom is just nostalgia and the best years are behind them. That is not a rule of life.

Nature offers a different image. As many deciduous leaves age, trees reclaim some of their valuable nutrients before the leaves fall. In some species, anthocyanins also develop during this process and may help protect the leaf. The lesson is not that autumn leaves are universally more valuable than summer leaves; it is that a later stage can still contribute something important.

In summer, I fought every comment. If someone said “your blog is wrong,” I would write 500 words reply. In autumn, I have learned — let the green fade. Let them see your gold. If they can’t see it, it’s their blindness, not your failure.

You don’t get wiser by adding colors. You get wiser by letting the loud green fade so the quiet gold can finally be seen.

— VGC Autumn Principle

Think of your father or your mother. When they were 35, they were busy, shouting, building. When they became 65, they became softer, funnier, they stopped arguing about small things. That is not necessarily weakness. In this metaphor, it is the color of experience: the wisdom that comes from living through things and no longer needing to prove yourself every day.

SUMMER BEHAVIOR

Chase sun. Prove worth. Say yes to everything. Fear that if I stop, tree will fall. Measure life in certificates, likes, salary slips.

AUTUMN BEHAVIOR

Reflect sun. Know worth. Say no without guilt. Know tree will stand even if I fall. Measure life in stories you can tell without getting angry.

3. The Controlled Detachment

The most VGC part of autumn is the abscission layer. The tree does not simply rip the leaf off. A specialized abscission layer develops near the base of the leaf stalk, and the timing varies with species and conditions. The process creates a natural separation point. While this is happening, the tree may reclaim nutrients from the leaf.

That is how wise people let go. Not ghosting. Not fighting. But a controlled detachment.

From a job that no longer respects you. From a friend circle that only calls when they need your help but makes ghunnus face when you grow. From the need to be liked by everyone. You don’t need to announce “I am detaching!” You just build your cork layer. Less supply. Less sugar to that branch. More sugar back to your own trunk for winter.

VGC Autumn Rule: If you are still chasing the sun with same urgency as summer, you will miss the beauty of transition. Autumn leaf no longer chases sun — it glows. Stop running. Start glowing. The race you are running — does it even have a finish line you want?
“Beauty is not only in growth. Beauty is also in the graceful, unapologetic reveal of what was hidden under all that striving.”— VGC Leaf Series, Phase 03

VGC Operator's Verdict: Phase 03

Autumn is not the beginning of the end. It can be the end of pretending. You stop pretending you are endlessly green. You admit you are tired, you are scarred, and you have colors you never showed because you were too busy photosynthesizing for others.

If you are feeling that chill — the feeling that you don’t want to hustle the same way — don’t panic. You are not falling behind. You are changing color. And that color — that amber, that crimson — carries a value that constant greenness can never fully express.

Let the abscission layer form. Give your last sugars back to your own roots. And get ready to dance — because next comes the most beautiful fall.
