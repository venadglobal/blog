The Foundation: Why We Punish the People Who Build Our Future

There is a massive hypocrisy in how society values education. If you ask anyone who has the most important job in the world, they will confidently say, "The people who teach our children."

✍️ Antony Ancil — Kollam, Kerala 📅 September 2026 ⏱️ 6 Minutes Read

There is a massive hypocrisy in how society values education. If you ask anyone who has the most important job in the world, they will confidently say, "The people who teach our children."

But if you look at how we actually pay and treat those people, a completely different reality emerges. We are living in a society that pays the absolute least to the people who build the foundation, and pays the most to the people who decorate the house.

"Let’s look at the raw reality of early childhood education versus higher education."

Who Actually Has the Harder Job?

There is a persistent myth that dealing with early childhood (pre-K and kindergarten) is "easy" because the subjects are simple. People look at it and think, It’s just alphabets, colors, and nap time. How hard can that be compared to teaching high school calculus?

"This is a fundamental misunderstanding of human development."

A university professor stands in front of a room of 18-year-olds who already know how to sit still, how to read, how to regulate their emotions, and how to interact with peers. The professor only has to transfer subject matter.

An early childhood educator is dealing with raw, unformed human psychology. They have a room full of 4-year-olds who have zero emotional regulation, no concept of sharing, and no understanding of social boundaries. The teacher is not just teaching the alphabet; they are teaching how to be a functioning human being.

Dealing with grown-up kids requires intellectual knowledge. Dealing with toddlers requires superhuman patience, constant physical energy, and deep psychological intuition. Teaching a teenager math is a job. Teaching twenty toddlers how to resolve conflict without biting each other is a miracle.

The Compensation Irony: Who Gets Paid More?

"Despite the immense physical and emotional toll of early education, society’s pay scale is completely inverted."

The High Earners: University professors, specialized high school tutors, and private coaches command respect, high salaries, and social prestige. We pay a premium to those who prepare teenagers for standardized tests or corporate jobs.

"The Lowest Earners: Early childhood educators are often paid wages that barely clear the poverty line."

Why? Because society financially rewards the complexity of the subject, not the complexity of the human. We respect the person who holds a Master's degree in Physics, but we view the person shaping a child's foundational psychology as a "glorified babysitter."

Who is Treated the Worst?

"Because early childhood educators are paid so poorly, their profession is deeply disrespected."

If a high school student fails a test, society blames the student for not studying. But if a four-year-old throws a tantrum, parents and society immediately blame the early childhood teacher for "lacking control." These educators are expected to parent, nurture, discipline, and educate dozens of children at once, all while being told their job is "just playing with kids."

"They face the highest burnout rates, the lowest budgets, and the least amount of societal gratitude."

Who is Actually Shaping the Future?

Psychology and neuroscience tell us that the most critical years of human development are between ages zero and five. This is when a child's brain forms the emotional and cognitive baseline they will carry for the rest of their lives.

If a child does not learn empathy, focus, and resilience in those early years, the best high school teachers in the world will not be able to fix it later. The early educators are the ones literally shaping the future of society. They are moulding the clay while it is still wet.

"Yet, we starve the foundation while throwing millions of dollars at the roof."

We need to stop pretending that teaching advanced physics is somehow more important than teaching a human being how to navigate the world with kindness and stability. It is time we start paying and respecting the actual architects of our society.
