The Ledger Always Balances:
Career Lessons in Power,
Loyalty, and Accountability

Whatever mistake gets made, the ledger eventually settles. What goes around comes around — even for the untouchable.

✍️ Antony Ancil — Kollam, Kerala 📅 August 2026 ⏱️ 4 min read

There's a familiar career arc that plays out in bureaucracies, police forces, and corporations everywhere. Someone rises to become the sharpest operator in the building — the enforcer, the fixer, the person leadership trusts to get the hard things done. For a while, they're untouchable.

Then, almost predictably, the ground shifts. A controversy breaks. Old alliances turn cold. The same system that once shielded them starts quietly stepping back. Strip away the specifics of any one case, and the pattern repeats itself often enough to be worth studying on its own terms.

1.

The Ascent — real skill, real usefulness, and a leadership team willing to look the other way on method as long as results keep coming.

2.

The Peak — outsized authority, protocols bent in their favor, very few people willing to challenge them directly.

3.

The Crack — one controversy, one accusation, one crack in the armor is enough to change the incentives for everyone watching.

4.

The Reckoning — the system that once needed them now needs distance from them, and quietly starts creating it.

Three operational lessons sit underneath that arc — useful for absolutely anyone, in absolutely any hierarchy.

1️⃣ The System Always Protects Itself, Not the Operator

This is close to the ultimate rule of bureaucracy and corporate life. You can be the sharpest tool in the shed, the most effective enforcer, leadership's clear favorite. But the moment your presence threatens the survival or public image of the system you serve, the system tends to cut you loose — regardless of how useful you were yesterday.

The lesson: never confuse temporary operational authority with permanent job security. You're valuable for as long as you're useful. Once you become a liability, the architecture that once protected you will move to protect itself instead.

2️⃣ The Danger of Over-Leveraging Power

When a single operator holds outsized power, bypasses standard protocol, and alienates peers along the way, they quietly build a silent list of people waiting for a crack in the armor. At the height of that power, almost nobody speaks up. But the moment someone finally does, information tends to surface fast — from people who'd simply been keeping quiet.

"True leadership requires building alliances, not just instilling fear. Rule purely by force and position, and the people under you will simply wait."

3️⃣ The "Front Man" Trap

In any large organization, the people at the very top rarely execute the hard, controversial, or ruthless decisions themselves. They rely on a trusted enforcer to carry them out. But when scrutiny arrives, the public and the media go after the visible face, not the invisible architect standing behind it.

The lesson: if you're executing someone else's controversial agenda, you're also carrying 100% of the operational risk attached to it. When the audit finally happens, it's your signature on the paper — not theirs.

The Venad Global Takeaway

Whether you're a senior officer in a police force, a manager in a Gulf corporation, or running a local project in Kollam, the underlying rule holds: never become bigger than the system you serve, and always keep your own exit clean.

What goes around, comes around.
The ledger always balances — sometimes just later than expected.
