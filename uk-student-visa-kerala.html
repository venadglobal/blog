🎓 UK Student Visa

Study in UK from Kerala —
Complete Student Visa Guide 2026

CAS, maintenance funds, IELTS requirements, real costs in Rs., Graduate Route post-study work visa, and the honest truth about what changed in 2026.

📅 May 2026 ✍️ Antony Ancil ⏱ 10 min read 🎓 Students 🇬🇧 UK

The UK remains one of the most popular study destinations for Kerala students. World-ranked universities, a two-year post-study work visa, and a clear pathway to permanent residence through the Skilled Worker route make it genuinely attractive.

But 2026 has brought important changes — visa fees have gone up, maintenance fund requirements have increased, and the Graduate Route post-study work period is being reduced from 2027. If your family is working with older information, this guide has the current figures.

This is the honest, complete picture. No sugarcoating.

Who This Guide Is For

  • Kerala students planning to study at a UK university (undergraduate or postgraduate)
  • Parents trying to understand the real costs involved
  • Students who want to understand the path from study to work to PR in the UK

Step 1 — Choose a University and Get Your CAS

Before you can apply for a UK student visa, you need a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS) — a unique reference number issued by your UK university. Without a CAS, there is no visa application.

1
Apply to a UK University and Receive an Offer
Apply through UCAS (ucas.com) for undergraduate courses, or directly to the university for postgraduate. You need to meet the academic entry requirements and the English language requirement of the institution.
2
Accept the Offer and Receive CAS
Once you accept your offer and pay any required deposit, the university issues your CAS number. The CAS includes details of your course, fees, and study dates. It is valid for 6 months from the date of issue — apply for your visa within this window.

Maintenance Funds — The 28-Day Rule

This is where many Kerala families get caught unprepared. You must show you have enough money to cover your first year's tuition fees PLUS living costs for up to 9 months. These funds must be in your bank account for 28 consecutive days before you apply.

⚠️ Critical — Read This Carefully

The 28-day rule means the money must be continuously in the account for 28 days before your visa application date. If you move money in and out — even briefly — the clock resets. Many Kerala visa rejections happen because families misunderstand this rule.

You also need to show first year tuition fees in addition to the maintenance funds. If your tuition is £20,000 per year, you need £20,000 + £10,224 (outside London) = £30,224 (~Rs.32 Lakh) sitting in the bank for 28 days. Plan early.

💡 Part-Time Work Rights

UK student visa allows you to work 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during holidays. At UK minimum wage of around £12.21/hour (2026), 20 hours per week brings in roughly £975/month (~Rs.1 Lakh) — meaningful contribution to living costs. Many Kerala students work in retail, hospitality, and care sectors.

After Graduation — The Graduate Route (Post-Study Work Visa)

This is the most important thing for Kerala students to understand — and it is changing.

Degree LevelCurrent (2026 graduates)From 2027 (new entrants)
Bachelor's Degree2 years post-study work18 months post-study work
Master's Degree2 years post-study work18 months post-study work
PhD3 years post-study work3 years (unchanged)
⚠️ Important Timing Note

A student starting in September 2026 and graduating in 2027 will fall under the NEW 18-month rule. A student who started in 2025 and graduates before the cutoff gets the full 2 years. If you are planning to start in 2026 — factor 18 months post-study into your planning, not 2 years.

Graduate Route Key Facts

  • No job offer needed to apply for Graduate Route visa
  • Can work at any skill level — not restricted to graduate roles
  • Can be self-employed
  • Application fee: approximately £880 (~Rs.94,000)
  • Immigration Health Surcharge: £1,035/year (~Rs.1.1 Lakh/year)
  • Must apply from inside the UK before Student visa expires

Pathway to Permanent Residence — The Full Journey

1
Student Visa — Duration of Course
Study your degree. Work part-time (20 hrs/week term time, full-time holidays). Build UK experience and network.
2
Graduate Route Visa — 18 Months (or 3 years for PhD)
Work in any role. Find a graduate-level job with a licensed sponsor. This is your window to secure a Skilled Worker visa.
3
Skilled Worker Visa — Up to 5 Years
Switch from Graduate Route to Skilled Worker visa. Requires a job offer from a licensed sponsor paying at least £41,700/year (or £33,400 for shortage occupations including many healthcare roles). This is where your long-term UK career begins.
4
Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR) — Permanent Residence
After 5 years on Skilled Worker visa, apply for ILR (permanent residence). Requires passing the Life in the UK test and meeting English language requirements. Time on Student visa does NOT count toward ILR — only Skilled Worker visa time counts.
5
British Citizenship
After 1 year of ILR, eligible to apply for British citizenship. Total minimum journey from Student visa to citizenship: approximately 8-10 years.
The Honest Timeline

Student visa (3-4 years) + Graduate Route (1.5 years) + Skilled Worker (5 years) + ILR (1 year to citizenship) = approximately 10-11 years from arrival to British citizenship. This is achievable but requires sustained commitment and planning at every stage.

Universities — Honest Advice for Kerala Families

UK universities are broadly categorised as:

  • Russell Group (24 universities) — Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial, UCL, Edinburgh, Manchester etc. Highly ranked, high fees (£25,000-35,000/year), better graduate employment outcomes. Harder to get into.
  • Post-92 Universities — more accessible entry requirements, lower fees (£12,000-18,000/year), more practical courses. Many excellent for nursing, business, engineering. Graduate outcomes vary — research carefully.
⚠️ Research Before You Pay

Some universities actively recruit Kerala students with easy admission offers. Before committing Rs.50+ Lakh to a UK degree, verify: Is the university on the UK's recognised bodies list? What are the graduate employment rates? Is the course professionally accredited (nursing by NMC, engineering by IMechE etc.)? A degree from a poorly ranked university with low employment outcomes is not worth Rs.50 Lakh of family money.

Final Word

The UK is genuinely a strong study destination for Kerala students — especially in healthcare, IT, and engineering where graduate employment is strong and the Skilled Worker pathway to permanent residence is well-established.

The most important things to remember:

  • The 28-day maintenance fund rule catches many families unprepared — plan finances early
  • Post-study work visa is reducing to 18 months from 2027 — factor this into your timeline
  • Research universities carefully — not all UK degrees deliver the same outcome
  • Start IELTS preparation early — 6-8 weeks is not enough for most students
  • The full journey from arrival to PR takes 10+ years — commit with clear eyes
