🎬 The Complete Journey

Three Films. One Family. Thirteen Years.

A cable TV operator with a Class 4 education outsmarts an entire police force to protect his family. The film that rewrote what Malayalam thriller could be. Budget ₹3.5 crore. Box office ₹62 crore. Remade in six languages.

Drishyam 2

★★★★½
2026
Drishyam 3

Released on Mohanlal's 66th birthday. New threats — old enemies — hidden enemies. Sahadevan returns for revenge. But the real danger was always closer than anyone suspected. Budget ₹100 crore.

★★★★½

I watched Drishyam 3 on May 22 — one day after release — at a noon show in Kollam. I came out with the same feeling I had after both the first and second films: that I had just watched something that understood Kerala in a way most films do not even attempt.

"Way, way much better and better always — Drishyam 3 is exceptional. And I think there will be a 4 too."

— Antony Ancil, Kollam, watching Day 2

What Drishyam 3 Is Really About

On the surface Drishyam 3 is a thriller about a family being hunted again — Sahadevan, the dismissed police officer, returning for revenge after the events of the first two films. But Jeethu Joseph is too intelligent a filmmaker to give us just that.

The real story is about what sustained deception does to a family's soul. Georgekutty protected his family in 1 and 2 with his intelligence. In 3, Jeethu Joseph asks a harder question: what happens to the people you protected? The mental health thread running through Drishyam 3 — centred on Rani and the daughters — is genuinely new territory for this franchise. Trauma does not disappear because the immediate threat is neutralised. It settles in the walls of the house.

And then there is Prabhakar — Varun's father — the hidden enemy that nobody saw coming. While everyone watched Sahadevan as the threat, the real manipulation was happening from somewhere else entirely. Georgekutty figures it out at the last minute — and that final act is why you need to watch this film twice to fully appreciate everything Jeethu Joseph planted.

Jeethu Joseph — The Architect

Let me be direct: Jeethu Joseph is one of the finest screenwriters working in Indian cinema today. The Drishyam series is not luck. It is craft — the craft of a man who has spent decades studying how ordinary people think under extraordinary pressure.

What makes his writing exceptional is restraint. Georgekutty never becomes a superhero. He never picks up a gun, never delivers a mass hero punch, never has a background score swelling to announce his brilliance. He thinks. He anticipates. He uses the simplest available materials — a cable TV schedule, a film screening, a hotel register — and turns them into an unbreakable alibi.

In Drishyam 3, Jeethu Joseph knew that his own pattern was exposed. Audiences had studied his method across two films. So he broke his own pattern deliberately — delivering an emotional shock at the end rather than the whistle moment everyone expected. That takes courage from a filmmaker.

"The script writer Jeethu Joseph is great and the entire team — special mention to each and everyone who worked hard."

— Antony Ancil, on the craftsmanship behind Drishyam 3
🎭 Character Study

Why Georgekutty Is Unlike Any Other Hero in Indian Cinema

Think of the great Indian film heroes. Most of them have a superpower — physical strength, special skills, extraordinary intelligence, supernatural ability. They are larger than life by design. The audience is meant to look up at them.

Georgekutty is the opposite. He is a Class 4 pass cable TV operator from a small Kerala town. His house is ordinary. His clothes are ordinary. His speech is ordinary. His only extraordinary quality is that he loves his family more than he fears consequences — and he thinks clearly when others panic.

That is why every Malayali recognises him. Not because they want to be him — but because they know someone like him. The father who quietly solves problems his family does not even know exist. The husband who absorbs pressure without showing it. The man who never asks for appreciation because he is too busy protecting.

"There are people who silently suffer and do everything for family — not like the movie narration, but the real truth underneath it. That is why it resonates."

— Antony Ancil, Kollam Kerala — on why Georgekutty touches the Malayali heart

That observation captures something no film review says plainly. The Drishyam films are not popular because they are thrilling — though they are. They are popular because they reflect a truth that hundreds of thousands of Kerala families live silently every day. The father who worked 30 years in the Gulf away from his children. The mother who managed everything alone. The family that holds together through pressure that would break most people — without dramatic music, without applause, without anyone filming it.

Jeethu Joseph found that truth and put Mohanlal inside it. That is the secret of Drishyam.

Mohanlal — The Complete Actor. There Is No Replacement.

Malayalam cinema has produced remarkable actors. But Mohanlal occupies a category of his own — and Drishyam 3 demonstrates exactly why on his 66th birthday.

The vulnerability level of Georgekutty in this third film is higher than in either of the first two. He is not the confident problem-solver of Drishyam 1. He is a man who has been carrying a secret for years and is beginning to feel its weight in ways he cannot fully control. Mohanlal conveys this without a single overplayed moment. The tension is in his eyes, in the slight hesitation before certain words, in the way he holds his body when someone asks the wrong question.

There is a softness to how Mohanlal renders his lines in this film that makes every word feel lived-in rather than performed. Dialogue that would sound theatrical from another actor sounds completely natural from him. That is a gift that cannot be taught — only refined over decades of genuine craft.

At 66, performing at this level, in a franchise that demands audiences compare him to his own peak performances — Mohanlal does not just hold his ground. He deepens the character. Georgekutty in 2026 is a richer, more complex, more vulnerable human being than Georgekutty in 2013. That growth is entirely Mohanlal's achievement.

"Mohanlal — the complete actor. I have been a fan from ages. There is no replacement."

— Antony Ancil, lifelong Mohanlal fan, Kollam Kerala
🌿 The Kerala Soul

Why These Films Belong Specifically to Kerala

The Landscape

Satheesh Kurup's cinematography in Drishyam 3 captures rural Kerala with extraordinary beauty — the light through rubber trees, the texture of red soil roads, the particular quality of afternoon light on a Kerala house. The landscape is not decoration. It is character. Georgekutty's intelligence is rooted in this specific place.

The Family Structure

The Georgekutty family dynamic — the quiet authority of the father, the resilience of Rani, the daughters navigating their own lives under the shadow of a secret — reflects something deeply authentic about how Kerala families actually function. Not the idealized version. The real one, with its pressures and its silences and its extraordinary loyalty.

The Gulf Connection

Many Malayalis watching Drishyam 3 bring their own family stories to the cinema. Fathers who were away for years. Families that held together through absence and pressure. When Georgekutty protects his family at any cost, every Kerala expat who ever sacrificed something for their family recognises that feeling immediately.

The Worldwide Reach

Drishyam was remade in Hindi, Telugu, Tamil, Kannada, Sinhala and Mandarin. The Hindi remake alone earned ₹345 crore. But every version was working from a blueprint drawn in Kerala, by a Kerala filmmaker, about a Kerala family. The original is always the deepest because it is the most specific — and specificity is what makes stories universal.

Is There a Drishyam 4?

After watching Drishyam 3, one thing is clear — Jeethu Joseph does not close doors he intends to open later. The final frames of the film leave threads deliberately unresolved. Esther Anil's Anu is being positioned for a larger role. The judicial system is still watching.

Mohanlal will be 69 when a Drishyam 4 could release. Georgekutty will be older, more tired, perhaps more vulnerable. That is not a weakness — that is the next chapter of the most honest family portrait in Malayalam cinema history.

If Jeethu Joseph writes it, Mohanlal will play it. And Kerala will fill every theatre on opening day. That much is certain.

Drishyam 3 — Watch It. Then Watch It Again.

A worthy conclusion to one of Indian cinema's finest trilogies. Not flawless — the first half tests patience. But the second half and climax deliver everything the franchise promised. Mohanlal at 66 reminds you why there is no replacement for the complete actor. Jeethu Joseph earned every rupee of that ₹100 crore budget.
