Summer: The Peak of Purpose — Why Maturity Means You Stop Growing For Yourself

The days grow long and golden. The leaf turns deep green, locks itself to the twig, and starts feeding the whole tree. That’s when real adulthood begins.

✍️ Antony Ancil — Kollam☀️ SUMMER SERIES🔗 PHASE 02October 2026
⚠️ VGC NOTE: Phase 2 of 4. If Phase 1 was about receiving support, Phase 2 is about turning what you have learned into contribution. The botanical examples are illustrative: leaf behaviour varies by species, season and growing conditions. Purpose also requires boundaries; contribution should not mean self-neglect.

There is a morning in May in Kerala when the mango leaves stop being light green and become that dark, almost arrogant green. You know that green? The one that looks like it owns the tree. That is summer. The leaf is no longer trying to become something. It has become. And now it has a job.

I remember my first summer, not in nature, but in Al Ain, working in a building where the chiller plant was my entire world. I was no longer, who just watches. I was given a logbook. “Antony, you write the readings now. If you write wrong, 300 people upstairs will sweat.” That was the first time I felt the weight of purpose. It was not about my growth anymore. It was about keeping others cool.

1. What Actually Happens in Summer

As many leaves mature, their expansion slows or stops. Their role increasingly shifts from building leaf area to supporting the plant through photosynthesis and the movement of sugars to other tissues. The timing differs by species and growing conditions.

As a leaf matures, its surface and internal tissues are adapted for their environment. A waxy cuticle helps limit water loss, while chlorophyll captures light energy for photosynthesis. The simplified equation is: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. Real leaves do not operate at “100% load”; photosynthesis changes with light, water, temperature, nutrients and other conditions.

Here is the systems point: a mature leaf does not use or store everything it produces. It can export a substantial share of its photosynthetic products through the phloem to other parts of the plant—such as roots, fruits, growing shoots and storage tissues. Research has reported exports around 50–80% in some mature leaves, but the proportion varies by species, conditions and the plant’s needs. The leaf supports parts of the tree it will never “meet”.

In human terms, this is adulthood. Not the Instagram adulthood of buying a car. The real one where you are the pillar. You wake up early not because you want to, but because someone depends on your logbook entry.

2. The Certification Trap — Stacking Leaves But Not Feeding the Tree

Across Kerala and the Gulf community, there can be pressure to collect certificates as proof of progress. IOSH, OSHA, NEBOSH, NFPA, PMP, and more — I have seen CVs packed with qualifications but offering little evidence of how the person applied that learning to protect a building, support a team or solve a real problem.

Summer leaf does not do that. It does not do 14 different types of photosynthesis. It does one photosynthesis, but does it so well that an entire branch lives because of it.

I tell my students: one strong, applied skill can be more valuable than ten certificates that never translate into practice. Qualifications matter, and I pursued them too—but ask: after you learned, who became safer, more capable or better supported because of you? If the answer is only “me”, there may still be a gap between collecting potential and applying it.

Potential drinks. Purpose exports. You know you have entered summer when 80% of your energy goes to people who will never thank you on LinkedIn.

Summer Principle

SPROUT MINDSET

How do I grow faster? How do I get noticed? How do I get more light for myself?

SUMMER MINDSET

How do I make more sugar than I need? Who needs shade today? What branch will fall if I don’t do my job?

3. Providing Shade — The Invisible KPI

One of the visible benefits of a leafy canopy is shade. It may not be the leaf’s only role, but it changes the conditions around the tree and the people beneath it.

Under a broad, healthy mango canopy in May, shade can make a real difference to how hot the space feels. The exact temperature difference is not fixed: it depends on canopy density, wind, humidity, soil moisture, time of day and what is being measured. The leaf never applied for a “shade provider” role. By doing its ordinary work, it helps create a more comfortable microclimate where children can play and workers can rest.

In facilities management, the best operators I have seen are like that. They are not loud. But when they are on shift, the building feels safe. Complaints go down. AC doesn’t trip. That is shade. No one writes a certificate for it, but everyone feels it.

At Venad Global, when I train safety officers, I tell them — your KPI is not your salary. Your KPI is how many people went home without sweating, without injury, because you did your reading correctly. That is shade. That is summer.

Healthy systems balance output with replenishment, boundaries and support. VGC Operator's Verdict: Phase 02

Summer is not comfortable. It is hot. It is high-pressure. It is pests, wind, and sunburn. But it is also the only season where you finally understand why you were fed so much in spring.

You received support, and now you have opportunities to support others. You were given shade; you can offer shade too. If you are in your 30s or 40s, working in the Gulf heat or building something while others doubt you, own your contribution—but remember that you also need rest, learning and support.

Don’t ask “when will I get my time?” This is your time. Not to shine for yourself, but to make sugar for the tree that held you.
