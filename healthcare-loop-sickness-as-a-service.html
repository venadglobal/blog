The Healthcare Loop —
Why the System Wants to Manage You, Not Cure You

The ultimate extraction machine: Trading human autonomy for Sickness as a Service (SaaS).

✍️ Antony Ancil — Kollam, Kerala 📅 August 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read

If you evaluate modern medicine through the lens of acute trauma, you have to admit that it is a miracle. If you shatter your leg in a car accident, require emergency surgery, or suffer an acute bacterial infection, the modern hospital is one of the greatest architectural achievements in human history.

The system is designed to save your life when you are hanging on the absolute brink of mortality.

But if you evaluate modern medicine through the lens of chronic illness, the miracle vanishes. Instead, you expose a deeply cynical, highly optimized corporate architecture. The modern healthcare system does not operate primarily as a healing mechanism; it operates on the exact same logic as a modern software company.

It is built on Recurring Revenue. Or, more accurately: Sickness as a Service (SaaS).

Here is the structural reality of the medical extraction machine, and why the system requires your continuous biological decline to maintain its profit margins.

💊 The Pharmaceutical Subscription Model

SYS_PROTOCOL // PHARMA_SUB STATUS: ACTIVE

Baseline Architecture: Curing a patient eliminates the customer. Killing the patient also eliminates the customer. The most profitable demographic in the world is a population that is continuously, mildly sick.

The system requires citizens to be sick enough to require daily pharmaceutical intervention, but alive enough to keep paying for it for decades.

We see this most clearly in the management of non-communicable diseases (NCDs) like Type-2 diabetes, hypertension, and metabolic syndrome.

If you take a computer with a corrupted hard drive to an IT specialist, they format the drive and reinstall the operating system. In human biology, formatting the drive means fixing the patient's toxic diet, eliminating sedentary behavior, and managing environmental stress.

But the system does not format the drive. Instead, it prescribes a daily pill to mask the warning lights on the dashboard. The underlying corruption is never fixed. The patient is placed on a "maintenance dose" of statins, insulin, or blood pressure medication, ensuring they become a lifelong subscriber to the pharmaceutical industry.

🍔 The Closed-Loop Extraction Engine

SYS_PROTOCOL // CLOSED_LOOP STATUS: EXTREME_RISK

Extraction Dynamics: Food conglomerates drain biological vitality → Systemic inflammation occurs → Pharmaceutical conglomerates step in to monetize the symptom management software patch.

To understand how this loop sustains itself, you have to look at the symbiotic relationship between the global food industry and the medical-industrial complex.

The recent Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) report revealed a staggering statistic: Over 56% of India’s total disease burden stems directly from unhealthy diets.

The food industry systematically pumps the population full of engineered, ultra-processed seed oils, refined sugars, and synthetic preservatives. These ingredients are mathematically designed to be hyper-palatable and highly addictive.

They create massive systemic inflammation, insulin resistance, and cellular damage. Once the food industry has successfully compromised your biological hardware, the pharmaceutical industry steps in to sell you the software patch.

The citizen is trapped directly in the middle of this closed loop: paying food conglomerates to drain their vitality, and paying pharmaceutical conglomerates to manage the symptoms.

It is the ultimate extraction machine, trading your physical autonomy for a double-sided revenue stream.

🌴 The Local Reality: Kerala’s Healthcare Paradox

We do not have to look to the West to see this system failing; the glitch is completely localized right here in Kerala.

Kerala is internationally praised for its health infrastructure and high life expectancy, but underneath the surface, it is a ticking time bomb of metabolic dysfunction. Recent data shows that Kerala has the highest prevalence of chronic diseases in India, standing at a massive 54%.

Look at the numbers from the Kerala Government’s ongoing 2026 'Shaili' digital health survey. Out of over 28 lakh adults screened recently, nearly 41% were found to have active risk factors for lifestyle diseases. The state is drowning in hypertension and diabetes.

We label these as "lifestyle diseases," which is a convenient way for the system to shift the blame onto the individual. But it is not just a failure of individual willpower; it is a failure of systemic design.

We have imported the Western diet of ultra-processed convenience, abandoned physical labor, and trapped ourselves in high-stress, sedentary environments. The medical community in Kerala is overwhelmed, but instead of demanding a radical overhaul of our food and lifestyle architecture, the state is simply building more pharmacies and dialysis centers. We are treating a structural collapse with Band-Aids.
