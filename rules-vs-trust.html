Rules vs Trust

The Fence and the Foundation.

✍️ Antony Ancil — Kollam, Kerala 📅 June 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read

People love talking about broken rules. Newspapers report them, courts adjudicate them, governments create new ones to replace them. But almost nobody talks about the deeper fracture that happens alongside — broken trust.

The two are not the same thing. Confusing them is one of the most common and costly mistakes people make — in relationships, in institutions and in life.

📜 Rules Are External — The Fence

Rules are artificial. They are created by society, companies and governments to maintain order, safety and predictability. They are structural — designed to manage behaviour from the outside.

⚠ The Nature of Rules
  • They are transactional — break a rule, pay the penalty, balance restored
  • They can be wrong — Rosa Parks broke a rule because the rule itself was unjust
  • They can be broken without malice — speeding to get someone to hospital
  • A rule tells you what you are allowed to do — nothing about who you are

Rules can be rewritten in an afternoon. A new policy, a new regulation, a revised clause. The mechanism for repair is built into the system itself.

🤝 Trust Is Internal — The Foundation

Trust is different in kind, not just in degree. It is psychological and personal — the willing choice to make yourself vulnerable to someone, believing they have your best interests at heart, or at minimum will not actively harm you.

✦ The Nature of Trust
  • It is deeply personal — about who someone is to you, not what they are permitted to do
  • Intent matters completely — breaking trust is never a technicality, it is a statement of character
  • The asymmetry of repair — years to build, seconds to break, sometimes a lifetime to repair
  • Even glued back together, the cracks remain visible

A broken rule can be corrected by policy. A broken trust can only be corrected by the person who broke it — and only if they choose to.

The Question Each One Forces

When a rule is broken
"Why did they do that?"
When trust is broken
"Who are they, really?"

That second question is far harder to answer — and far harder to live with once asked. It does not go away after a penalty is paid or an apology is given. It sits in every future interaction, quietly, waiting to be confirmed or disproved.

Why This Matters More Than Society Admits

Modern systems are obsessed with rule-making. New policies, new compliance frameworks, new regulations — each one promising better order. Every scandal produces a new committee. Every failure produces a new rule.

But rules mean almost nothing if the underlying trust is already dead.

Trust is the actual operating system.
Rules are just the documentation written on top of it.

If you do not trust a person — or an institution — no number of rules will make you feel safe around them. You will read every clause looking for the trick. You will assume the worst interpretation. You will protect yourself rather than engage openly.

A relationship, a workplace, a government — none of them survive on rules alone. They survive on whether the people inside them believe the other party is genuinely acting in good faith.

When that belief is gone, no rulebook, however detailed, fills the gap.
