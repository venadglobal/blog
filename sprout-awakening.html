The Sprout: The Awakening — Why Your Life Begins Long Before You Notice It

Before you hustle, before you shine, before you even know your own name — you are soft, blind, and completely held by roots you cannot see.

✍️ Antony Ancil — Kollam 🌸 SPRING SERIES 🔗 INTERCONNECTED
⚠️ VGC NOTE: This is Phase 1 of 4 of The Leaf & The Life series. Each phase is a human audit — not motivation. We look at how nature actually works, then ask: where am I in this cycle? For public awareness and reflection.

It begins in a silence so quiet you would miss it if you were in a hurry. On a branch that looks still, a tiny bud begins to swell. Not because someone told it to. Not because it is trending. A combination of the plant’s internal signals and its surroundings has made the next stage possible. The change begins before the world notices.

I used to ignore this part. Honestly. I thought life starts when you start doing things — when you get a job, when you start a company, when you write your first blog and a few people from other parts of the world read it. That’s when life starts, right?

No. Life starts much before that. In a place where no one is clapping for you.

1. What Actually Happens in The Sprout

In botany, bud break is not an explosion. It is a response to conditions. The details vary by species, climate and season; there is no single switch that makes every tree wake up.

In many trees from seasonal or temperate climates, a bud’s return to growth can depend on the amount of cold it has experienced, later warmth, day length and the tree’s own biology. Roots absorb water and nutrients, while stored resources help support early growth. But a soil temperature of 8–10°C is not a universal trigger, and root pressure alone does not explain bud break. Different species follow different cues.

In many woody plants, a bud forms before it opens and protective scales shelter the developing tissues. When growth resumes, cells expand and divide, and the young leaves gradually emerge. Water uptake is part of plant growth, but it would be misleading to say every bud follows one fixed sequence or that imbibition alone starts the process. Soft. Silent. No drama.

There is no reliable universal 48–72-hour timetable for cell division and leaf opening; the pace depends on species and conditions. The leaf unfurls not like a flag, but like a child rubbing his eyes after sleep — slow, crinkled, slightly confused by the light.

That’s the audit. A mature tree does not begin each new flush as a finished leaf. New growth starts vulnerable and draws on support already built into the system. It starts soft, before it can contribute.

2. The Human Equivalent — We Are All Sprouts Once

Birth and early childhood. That’s what this phase is in human language.

Think about it. When you were 2 years old, you had no ambition, no LinkedIn, no plan to become something. You were just… potential. A bundle of soft, vibrant, untapped possibility. You didn’t know the world was vast, or cruel, or beautiful. You just knew there was a lap. There were hands. There was milk. There was someone staying awake so you could sleep.

That is the root system. In humans, roots are not in the soil. They are people. Amma. Appa. That one teacher in LP school who said you write well. That uncle in Dubai who sent 500 rupees. You don’t remember their names some days, but their sap is still in you.

I met a guy in Kollam last month — 22, wants to be a safety officer. He told me, “Sir, I have nothing.” I told him, you have everything. You are just in sprout phase. You are drinking. Everyone wants to skip drinking and go straight to photosynthesis. Doesn’t work like that.

You don’t become strong and then get support. You get support and then you become strong. The sprout gets the sap first, does the work later.

— VGC Human Systems Principle #1

Our generation has a problem. We hate being dependent. We think dependency is weakness. But in living systems, dependence is often part of the design. A young shoot draws on resources and protection before it can contribute much in return. That is not a bug — it is one way a living system carries growth through a vulnerable stage.

If you are in your early 20s, restarting life at 40 after returning from the Gulf, or starting a blog while people around you respond with doubt — you may be in your sprout phase. Don’t be ashamed of needing help. Don’t be in a hurry to look green and big.

3. The Hidden Work No One Likes to Talk About

Here is what Instagram will never show you: the sprout phase is ugly for a long time.

For days, the bud just looks swollen. Like something is wrong with it. People walk under the tree and think nothing is happening. Same with you. You are learning. You are reading manuals. You are failing IOSH mock tests. You are writing blogs that 3 people read. From outside, it looks like nothing is happening.

But inside, learning is happening. You are taking in knowledge, patterns, feedback and experience. You are drinking, even when the growth is not yet visible.

In my own case — 36+ years in facilities management across the UAE and India — the sprout phase included those first three years of not knowing which spanner was which, standing behind senior colleagues and watching. I thought I was wasting time. Now I see that I was absorbing the basics. Without that foundation, later responsibility would have been much harder to carry.

WHAT IT FEELS LIKE

Soft, vulnerable, slow. You ask a lot of questions. You say “I don’t know” more than “I know.” You feel like everyone else is already in summer while you are still wrapped in scales.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY HAPPENING

Quiet learning. Stronger connections. Protective boundaries that keep you from taking on more than you are ready for. You are building the foundations that can support later growth.

Rule for Sprout Phase: Don’t tear your own protective layers off too early. Don’t expose yourself to every pressure just to prove you are mature. In nature, early growth can be vulnerable to harsh conditions; in life, too much exposure before you have the skills and support to handle it can also do harm. Stay protected while you prepare — but remember that growth eventually requires light, practice and measured risk.

Translation: Don’t take advice from everyone. Don’t post every small win for validation. Don’t quit your learning job because someone on YouTube said “9-to-5 is slavery.” You need that 9-to-5 sap right now.

4. The Test — Can You Stay Soft in a Hard World?

The final test of the sprout is not strength alone. It is the ability to stay flexible under pressure without abandoning the work of growing.

The wind will come when you are still unfurling. A newly emerged mango leaf can be tender and vulnerable to harsh weather. The leaf does not argue with the wind. It moves. It bends. Its flexibility is part of how it meets the conditions around it.

As humans in the sprout phase, our job is not to argue with every doubter, prove everyone wrong, or keep checking who has read our work. Our job is to stay open, keep learning and choose carefully whose feedback deserves our attention.

That is the real awakening. Not when the world notices you. But when you notice the roots that have been holding you all along and you finally say — okay, I am ready to take this light.

“A leaf never asks if it deserves the sun. It just, after a long winter of being fed in the dark, opens. And that opening itself is a kind of prayer.”— VGC Leaf Series, Phase 01

VGC Operator's Verdict: Phase 01

The sprout teaches us that beginnings are not about performance. They are about conditions. You are allowed to need support. You are allowed to be unseen. You are allowed to spend time learning before you show the world visible results. Nature does not follow a universal 72-hour timetable, and neither does human growth.

If you rush this phase, you may build a fragile appearance without the foundation to sustain it. If you respect it, you give your skills, judgment and confidence time to develop — so that one day your growth can also offer shade to others.

So ask yourself tonight — where are you still pretending to be in summer, when actually you are in spring? Go back to drinking. Go back to roots.
