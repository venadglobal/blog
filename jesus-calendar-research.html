The Central Question

Jesus stated clearly in Matthew 12:40: "For as Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of a huge fish, so the Son of Man will be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth."

This is the Sign of Jonah — the central prophecy Jesus gave about his own death and resurrection. Yet by the Gregorian calendar commonly used today, the traditional Good Friday to Easter Sunday timeline produces at most 1.5 to 2 days — not 3 full days and 3 full nights. Similarly, a Thursday Last Supper with Friday crucifixion only yields 2 nights and parts of days — still insufficient.

How do we resolve this? The answer lies entirely in understanding which calendar was in use in Israel at the time of Jesus — and how a "day" was defined in that calendar.

"And there was evening, and there was morning — the first day."
Genesis 1:5 — The Biblical definition of a day

Why The Last Supper Was Tuesday Evening

The Last Supper was the Pesach (Passover) meal — eaten at the beginning of Nisan 14 (when Nisan 13 ended at sunset on Tuesday). Jesus and the disciples ate the Passover meal, after which Judas left to betray him. The arrest, the trial before Caiaphas, and the night of suffering all happened on Tuesday night — which in Hebrew reckoning was already Wednesday (Nisan 14). This means the crucifixion on Wednesday daytime was still on Nisan 14 — the correct Passover day when the lamb must be sacrificed.

This is confirmed by 1 Corinthians 5:7 — "Christ our Passover has been sacrificed" — Jesus died at 3PM on Nisan 14, precisely when the Passover lambs were being slaughtered at the Temple.

Why Were There Two Sabbaths?

This is one of the most misunderstood aspects of the Crucifixion timeline — and one of the strongest proofs that the traditional Friday-Sunday timeline is incorrect.

There were two separate Sabbaths in the week of the Crucifixion:

SabbathDateScriptural Reference
High Sabbath (Annual) Nisan 15 — First day of the Feast of Unleavened Bread. Falls on a fixed date regardless of the day of week. In AD 30 it fell on Thursday night/Friday. John 19:31 — "that Sabbath was a HIGH DAY"
Weekly Sabbath The regular Saturday Sabbath — Nisan 16 in AD 30. The women rested on this day and then went to the tomb on Sunday morning. Luke 23:56 — "they rested on the Sabbath"

Mark 16:1 states the women "bought spices after the Sabbath" — this was after the High Sabbath on Thursday night — before the weekly Saturday Sabbath. They then rested again on Saturday per Luke 23:56, and came to the tomb early Sunday. This only makes sense if there were two Sabbaths.
